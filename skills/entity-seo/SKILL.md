# Entity SEO — brand, product, and author identity in search knowledge systems

Makes a brand, its products, and its authors recognizable as distinct entities in search engines' knowledge systems — the entity-strategy and topical-authority layer, deciding which identity signals to ship, not writing the markup.

## Conditions

### When to use

- Deciding the entity strategy for a brand, its products, and its authors: which identity signals to ship so search and AI systems recognize each as one distinct entity.
- Planning stable-@id Organization and Person identity, cross-touchpoint name/logo/description consistency, and the sameAs / Wikidata signals that corroborate an entity.
- Triggers: "entity SEO", "entity optimization", "Knowledge Graph", "Knowledge Panel", "brand entity", "entity signals", "entity linking", "how do I make my brand a recognized entity", "topical authority".

### When NOT to use

- Writing the actual JSON-LD (Organization, Person, or any other type) — that is the implementation reference seo.
- Mapping which page type carries which schema across a site, or building the E-E-A-T layer — that build work is seo-plan's; entity signals support E-E-A-T but are not the build layer.
- Planning AI-search citation strategy (per-engine selection logic, content blocks, the citation ladder) — that is geo-plan. This document supplies only the entity signals that strengthen citation.
- Auditing a live site or scoring a deployment — this skill plans entity strategy and writes an artifact; it fetches and scores nothing.

### Prerequisites (what you must supply, and who cannot)

This skill needs three inputs, and it is honest about who cannot produce them:

- **The identity facts.** The exact canonical brand name, the legal / organization name, each product name, and each author's name, credentials, and profile URLs. These come from the brand owner, marketing, or founder — not from the web. Without them there is no entity to make recognizable, and this is the one input no tool can substitute.
- **Control over site-wide markup.** Shipping Organization and Person schema means injecting JSON-LD into the root layout or a global template (`layout.tsx`, `_document`, a global header, or a WordPress/theme template). Deploy access to that global surface is required. On a locked site builder that will not serve custom markup, you can still do the identity-facts and off-site-consistency work, but the site-wide schema waits until you have markup access — then hand the implementation to seo.
- **Control over off-site profiles.** Cross-touchpoint consistency and sameAs corroboration require the ability to edit the brand's social accounts, directory listings, and — where the entity is genuinely notable — Wikidata / Wikipedia. These come from whoever owns those accounts.

One dead-end stated up front: **most sites cannot directly obtain a Knowledge Panel.** It is generated by Google from Wikidata, Wikipedia, and broad third-party consensus, not from anything you add to your own site (see Knowledge Panel reality). If the goal is "get us a Knowledge Panel", the honest path is strengthening the entity signals that make one more likely over time — not a deliverable this or any skill can produce on demand.

### What breaks first

- The dated facts in ## Era. Search engines and AI surfaces evolve; if you are reading this well after the last-verified date, re-check the era pins before trusting the strategy.
- The AI-search roster and behavior. Which engines cite, and how they resolve entities, rotate quickly — treat the 2026 per-engine picture as a snapshot and defer the live detail to geo-plan.
- Knowledge Panel obtainability and Google's "claim this knowledge panel" flow are Google-controlled and change without notice.

## Era

Reviewed 2026-08-10 (batch 940); this document targets how Google and AI-search engines model entities as of August 2026.

Entity SEO rests on one dated shift: Google moved from matching keyword strings to understanding meaning and the entities behind a query. That shift is a real, datable progression, and it — not any visibility multiplier — is the citable era marker here:

- **Hummingbird (2013)** rebuilt the ranking engine around query meaning rather than individual keywords.
- **RankBrain (2015)** added a machine-learning layer that interprets unseen and ambiguous queries by intent.
- **BERT (2019)** brought bidirectional language understanding, reading a word in the context of the words on both sides of it.
- **MUM (2021)** extended that understanding across languages and modalities.

Together these established the Knowledge Graph — Google's database of entities and their relationships — as central to how queries are resolved and disambiguated. As of 2026, AI-search surfaces reward clearly-identified entities: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Claude all name and cite sources, and a brand, product, or author that reads as one unambiguous entity is easier for them to identify and cite than one that does not. Clear entity signals improve how AI systems identify and cite a brand; there is no reliable published multiplier for that effect, and any specific "N× visibility" figure circulating in marketing posts is undated and unsourced — this document makes no such claim.

## The entity-audit artifact

Everything this skill produces goes into one file, `entity-audit.md`, in the working directory. The Verify blocks below grep that file — never a live site — so its shape is load-bearing:

```markdown
# Entity Audit — [brand]

## Identity facts
- canonical brand name: [the one exact name used everywhere]
- legal / organization name: [if different from the brand name]
- product names: [each product as its own entity]
- authors: [name -> credentials -> profile URL], one per bylined author
- authoritative URLs: home, about (the entity home)

## Identity gaps
- [brand | product | author]: [the ambiguity or inconsistency found]

## Schema @id plan
- Organization @id: https://example.com/#organization   (ships in: root layout, every page)
- Person @id: https://example.com/author/<slug>/#person
- WebSite <-> Organization link: [yes/no]

## sameAs / off-site profiles
- [social, directory, and Wikidata/Wikipedia URLs that corroborate the entity]

## Consistency checklist
- [ ] name identical across site, social, directories
- [ ] logo identical across touchpoints
- [ ] description aligned across touchpoints

## Knowledge Panel
- existing panel: [yes/no]
- claimable: [yes, via Google "claim this knowledge panel" / no]
- entity home: [the authoritative About URL]
```

Two rules for the artifact. Every identity you care about — the brand, each product, each author — gets an entry with the exact name it should carry everywhere. And every `@id` is a stable, absolute URL with a fragment, so the same entity is referenced by the same identifier across every page.

## Entity vs keyword

An entity is a singular, well-defined thing — a person, place, organization, product, or event — that keeps one identity across every context. A keyword is a text string, and strings are ambiguous: "Apple Inc." and "apple (the fruit)" share a word but are different entities. Entity SEO is the work of making search engines able to identify, categorize, and connect your brand, products, and authors as such entities, each with its own identity, attributes (founding date, location, category), and relationships to other entities. Where keyword work chases phrases, entity work fixes an unambiguous identity that holds whatever words a query uses.

Record each entity you want recognized under the artifact's Identity facts, and note where its name, category, or relationships are currently ambiguous under Identity gaps.

### Verify

```bash
grep -qiE "canonical brand name" entity-audit.md || echo "FAIL: no canonical brand name recorded in Identity facts"
grep -qiE "^## Identity gaps" entity-audit.md || echo "NOTE: no Identity gaps section — confirm there is genuinely no ambiguity to resolve"
```

## Entity signals to ship

The signals that make an entity recognizable are mostly about identity and consistency, not markup volume:

| Signal | What it does |
|---|---|
| One canonical brand/product name | Prevents confusion with similarly named entities; the same exact name everywhere |
| Author identity | A real person behind bylined content — bio, credentials, a linked author page |
| Organization identity | Organization schema shipped site-wide, with logo and sameAs |
| Self-contained, citable paragraphs | Each block understandable alone, so an AI system can extract it with the entity intact |
| Cross-touchpoint consistency | The same name, description, and logo across website, social, and directories |

The two schema types that carry entity identity — Organization and Person — are covered next. For the full page-type schema catalogue and the actual JSON-LD, this document defers to other bundle members rather than re-teaching them.

### Verify

```bash
grep -qiE "authors?:" entity-audit.md || echo "NOTE: no author identity recorded — add one if the site has bylined content"
grep -qiE "sameAs" entity-audit.md || echo "FAIL: no sameAs profiles planned — the core off-site entity signal is missing"
```

## Organization and Person identity

Two schema types anchor an entity in the Knowledge Graph, and both turn on a stable `@id`:

- **Organization** identifies the brand. Ship it site-wide — from the root layout or a global component so it appears on every page, not confined to the About page — with a stable `@id` (for example `https://example.com/#organization`), plus `name`, `url`, `logo`, and a `sameAs` list. Link Organization to WebSite on the home page. Use the most specific type that fits (LocalBusiness, SoftwareApplication, and so on).
- **Person** identifies an author. Give each author a stable `@id` (for example `https://example.com/author/jane/#person`), `name`, `url`, `affiliation`, and `sameAs` to their real profiles.

The point of the fragment `@id` is entity linking: the same identifier, reused across pages, tells a parser these references are all the one entity. Decide the `@id` strategy and which identities ship here; the seam with the rest of the bundle is deliberate:

- For the actual JSON-LD blocks — Organization, Person, and every other page-type schema — hand off to the implementation reference **seo**.
- For which page type carries which schema across a whole site, that per-page mapping is **seo-plan**'s build layer.
- Entity signals support E-E-A-T; the E-E-A-T build layer itself (authorship surfaces, trust signals, the current Quality Rater edition) is **seo-plan**'s, not this document's.

### Verify


## Cross-touchpoint consistency

An entity is only as recognizable as it is consistent. Search and AI systems corroborate an entity by seeing the same name, description, and logo across many surfaces; contradictions dilute the signal. Fix one canonical name, one description, and one logo, and carry them identically across the website, every social profile, and third-party directories. Nominate one authoritative page — usually the About page — as the entity home that every other reference points back to, and align sameAs links both ways so the profiles corroborate each other. Where a brand spans multiple domains or sub-brands, the same rule holds: one consistent identity, or the entity fractures into several weaker ones.

### Verify

```bash
grep -qiE "name identical|same name" entity-audit.md && grep -qiE "logo" entity-audit.md \
  || echo "FAIL: consistency checklist missing name or logo parity"
grep -qiE "entity home|authoritative about" entity-audit.md || echo "NOTE: no entity home nominated — pick one authoritative page"
```

## Knowledge Panel reality

A Knowledge Panel is the entity box Google shows in the SERP for a brand, person, or place. Be honest about what you can and cannot do here, because this is where entity work is most often oversold:

- **Most sites cannot directly obtain a Knowledge Panel.** Google generates it from its own Knowledge Graph — sourced from Wikidata, Wikipedia, licensed data, and broad third-party consensus — not from anything you add to your own site. There is no markup, file, or request that produces one on demand.
- **If a panel already exists** for your entity, you can claim it (Google's "claim this knowledge panel" flow, after identity verification) and suggest corrections — but claiming is not creating.
- **What you can actually do** is strengthen the entity signals that make a panel more likely over time: a consistent identity everywhere, an authoritative entity home, corroborating sameAs profiles, and — where the entity is genuinely notable — accurate Wikidata and Wikipedia presence.

Record the panel's real status in the artifact rather than promising one.

### Verify

```bash
grep -qiE "existing panel|claimable|cannot (directly )?obtain|wikidata|wikipedia" entity-audit.md \
  || echo "FAIL: Knowledge Panel reality not recorded (existence, claimability, or the signals behind it)"
grep -niE "(guarantee|will (get|obtain|create)).{0,30}knowledge panel" entity-audit.md \
  && echo "REVIEW: a Knowledge Panel is being promised — it cannot be directly obtained"
```

## Entity signals that strengthen AI citation

AI-search engines name and cite sources, and a clearly-identified entity is easier to name and cite than an ambiguous one. The entity contribution to citation is specific and narrow: a consistent identity, self-contained paragraphs that carry the brand / product / author name intact when lifted out of the page, and corroborating presence across the wider web (site, social, forums, Wikipedia) so an engine keeps resolving the same entity. Clear entity signals improve how AI systems identify and cite a brand — that is the honest claim, with no visibility multiplier attached.

This is the entity layer only. The full AI-search citation build — per-engine selection logic, content blocks, the citation ladder, and what actually earns a citation — is **geo-plan**'s job; strengthen the entity signals here and hand the citation strategy to geo-plan.
